Can You Afford Denmark?

Housing prices across Denmark have risen dramatically since 2008 — but incomes haven't kept pace everywhere. This project investigates a widening divide: between city and countryside, between those who got in early and those priced out entirely.

Part 1 — The Guided Story

A Nation Divided
by Housing Costs

We start from a simple question: how easily can a typical Danish household afford housing where they live? We measure this with an Affordability Index — income divided by the average price per square metre. A higher number means housing is more affordable relative to local income.

What we found tells a story of growing inequality — not just between rich and poor, but between where you happen to live.

Data

About the Dataset

This project combines three datasets covering all Danish municipalities from 2008 to 2024:

Housing transactions — approximately 1.5 million sales records from Boligsiden, aggregated to municipality-year level. Variables include purchase price, sqm price, property size, number of rooms, and the difference between offer and sale price.

Income data — mean municipal income from Statistics Denmark, available annually per municipality (~1,600 rows).

Population data — annual population counts per municipality from Statistics Denmark.

Macro variables (interest rates, inflation, mortgage bond yields) are national-level figures from Danmarks Nationalbank, merged onto the dataset by year.
